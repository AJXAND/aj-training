# Water polo: research note

## Demands
- Highly intermittent. International women's match play: mean exercise bout 7.4 s, work:rest about 1:1.6. Time split: swimming 64%, contested swimming 13%, wrestling 14%, holding position 9% (D'Auria & Gabbett 2008).
- Position matters. Centre players wrestle more (18.4% vs 9.9% of time); outside players swim more (67.5% vs 60.2%) and do more maximal or near-maximal swims per quarter (1.2 vs 0.5) (D'Auria & Gabbett 2008).
- Defensive system changes drill intensity (press: HR 153 bpm, lactate 6.5 mmol/L vs zone 140-143 bpm, about 4.6-4.7 mmol/L) but not whole-game mean HR (Botonis et al. 2015).
- Goalkeepers: mean HR 134 bpm; 86% of play below the anaerobic threshold, 14% in sudden bursts above it; lactate about 3.9 mmol/L at the end of each period (J Sci Med Sport 2009, 12:244-250; author not shown on the record I could open).
- Strength links: in elite German youth squad players (n=15, mean age 16), throwing velocity 68.5 km/h (range 60-75), 400 m freestyle 5:48 (5:14-6:27). Lab strength correlated with in-water tests at r=0.52-0.70; adductor, abdominal and pectoral strength correlated with eggbeater endurance and agility (r=0.53-0.66). In-water jump height tracked arm length most (r=0.89) (Zinner et al. 2015).
- Energy: aerobic base carrying repeated short, high-intensity swims, sprints and wrestling.

## Injuries
- Systematic review of 41 studies: head, fingers and shoulders are the most frequent sites. Traumatic injuries hit hands and head (ball and opponent contact); training injuries mainly hit the shoulder (Croteau/Pearsall et al. 2021).
- Shoulder injury rates 24-51% across studies, and shoulder problems are more likely to become chronic than other water polo injuries. Proposed risk factors: high shooting volume, range of motion, scapular dyskinesis, strength imbalance, proprioception, throwing kinematics (Miller et al. 2018).
- Prospective cohort (76 sub-elite players): later shoulder injury linked to lower pre-season ER strength (11.7% vs 14.5% body weight), lower IR strength (16.5% vs 21.6%), and a dominant total rotation range loss. ER strength at or below 12.5% BW: OR 5.2 (wide CI) (Hams et al. 2019). A second cohort found pre-season absolute IR and ER strength lower in players later injured (Scand J Med Sci Sports 2019).

## Prevention
- No water polo prevention RCT found. Closest evidence: OSTRC shoulder programme in elite handball, 28% fewer shoulder problems (Andersson 2017, already in Part 0). Graded moderate by extrapolation.
- Priorities: shoulder ER and IR strength, internal rotation range (sleeper, cross-body), scapular control, thoracic rotation, shot-count management (practice), adductor strength for eggbeater (practice, supported by the Zinner correlations).

## Positions
- Centre forward (hole set) and centre back: most wrestling and holding; strength, mass, trunk and pulling.
- Field players (drivers, wings, point): most maximal swims; repeat sprint swim capacity and throwing power.
- Goalkeeper: lower average intensity, explosive eggbeater lifts and lateral lunges, leg and hip strength.

## Season (UK amateur, practice)
- BWPL and regional leagues run roughly autumn to spring. OFF late spring to early summer, PRE late summer, IN autumn to spring. Club training is mostly pool time, so gym time is limited to 2-3 sessions.

## Tests with norms
- Shot speed (radar or app): elite youth men mean 68.5 km/h, range 60-75 (Zinner 2015). Bands are practice built on that.
- 400 m freestyle: elite youth men 5:48 (5:14-6:27) (Zinner 2015).
- Shoulder ER strength %BW (handheld dynamometer): flag at or below 12.5% (Hams 2019).
- Dominant shoulder total rotation range loss: flag at or above 7.5 deg (Hams 2019).
- Land CMJ and pull-up reps: practice bands.

## Evidence limits
- Mostly descriptive and small cohorts. Prevention is extrapolated from handball. Confidence: moderate.
