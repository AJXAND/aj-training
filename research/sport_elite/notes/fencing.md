# Fencing: research note

## Demands
- Poule fights: first to 5 or 3 min. Direct elimination (DE): first to 15 or 3 x 3 min with 1 min rest. Tokyo 2020 DE times: épée 16:39, foil 18:21, sabre 11:31 (Oates, Price, Bottoms 2023).
- Work per point: épée about 15 s, foil about 5 s, sabre about 2.5 s. Work:rest: épée about 1:1 to 2:1, foil about 1:1 to 1:3, sabre about 1:5 to 1:6 (Oates 2023).
- HR averages 75-100% HRmax; simulated épée peak 92-96% HRmax. Lactate usually below 4 mmol/L, but about 7 after a DE has been reported. Competition VO2 averages 56-74% of VO2max. Core temperature reached 38.4 C after DEs, above 39 C in some (Oates 2023).
- Movement (simulated épée): about 75-78 m/min, peak speed 3.4-3.9 m/s, mostly low to moderate intensity with short explosive bursts (Oates 2023).
- Lunge velocity averaged 3.35 m/s. Standing broad jump was the strongest predictor of lunge velocity (r=0.51) and change-of-direction speed (r=-0.65). Rear leg drive 14.6 N/kg, front leg impact about 3 x body weight. Single-leg jump asymmetry 9 +/- 8% favouring the front leg (Turner et al. 2016).
- Review: lunging and change-of-direction speed underpin performance, supported by strength and power; hamstrings should be strengthened; the asymmetric stance needs balancing work (Turner et al. 2014).
- Turner's group found no physiological or biomechanical differences large enough to need weapon-specific S&C (reported in the Middlesex GBR case study). Weapon changes conditioning work:rest, not the gym.

## Injuries
- FIE international competitions 2010-2014: 0.28 time-loss injuries per 1,000 athlete-exposures. Lower limb 72.4%; ankle sprain the single most common injury (25.3%); sprains 40.8%, strains 20.1%; 47% non-contact from the fencer's own movement; median 4 weeks lost. Men RR 1.42 vs women. Épée about half the risk of foil and sabre (Harmer 2019).
- Meta-analysis (19 studies): prevalence 51.5-92.8% in able-bodied fencers. Moderate-quality risk factors: older age, more years fencing, Functional Movement Screen below 15. Very low quality: male sabre, higher level (Wong et al. 2025).

## Prevention
- No fencing-specific RCT found. Use ankle balance training (strong evidence in other sports), Nordic hamstring (strong, football), adductor and hip work for the deep lunge, patellar tendon load management for the front knee (practice), and bilateral strength to reduce asymmetry (practice, Turner 2014).

## Variants
- Foil: short actions, about 1:1 to 1:3.
- Épée: longest actions and most continuous movement; lowest injury rate.
- Sabre: shortest, most explosive actions; higher injury risk than épée.

## Season (UK, practice)
- Domestic circuit runs roughly September to June with weekend tournaments; summer is the main off-season. Fencers peak for 2-4 key events.

## Tests with norms
- Standing broad jump: best single predictor (Turner 2016). Bands are practice estimates.
- Single-leg CMJ or hop asymmetry: elite mean 9% (Turner 2016); flag above 15% (practice).
- Y-balance anterior reach difference above 4 cm (Plisky 2006, Part 0).
- CMJ: practice bands.
- Lunge velocity 3.35 m/s average in elite pathway fencers, needs video or sensors (Turner 2016).

## Evidence limits
- Good competition injury surveillance and some performance work from the GBR programme; no prevention trials. Confidence: moderate.
