# Orienteering: research note

## Demands
- Foot orienteering: sprint (12-15 min, urban/parks), middle (~30-35 min), long (60-100 min) in forest; relays. Rogaining and adventure racing are 6-24 h+ navigation events, often multi-sport.
- Elite VO2max up to ~76 (men) and ~63 ml/kg/min (women); mean race HR ~167-172 bpm; forest terrain raises oxygen cost ~26% versus road; leg strength generally modest (Creagh & Reilly 1997).

## Injuries
- Elite one-year prospective (42 runners): 1.7 injuries per runner per year; 52% acute, 48% overuse. Ankle sprains 37% of acute injuries; overuse: medial shin pain, Achilles, peroneal tendons, IT band, all lower limb (Linde 1986).
- Jukola/Venla relays (9,724 runners): 2.5% injured; 15.4 per 1,000 competition hours; ankle sprains 25%; ~70% lower limb; women more injured (Linko 1997).
- Competition rates 7-15 per 1,000 h vs 2-3 in training; 70-94% lower extremity; risk factors in juniors: previous injury, high volume, running on asphalt (summary in von Rosen & Halvarsson 2018 protocol).

## Prevention (graded)
- RCT in Swedish elite orienteers: 4 exercises (heel raises, runner's pose, single-leg stance, side hops), 10 min, 4x/wk. Intention to treat: no significant effect (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.19-1.34). Done at least twice a week: OR 0.25 (0.06-0.97) for being substantially injured (Halvarsson & von Rosen 2019). Grade: moderate.
- Ankle balance training reduces sprains in other sports (strong; shared rules). Previous sprain: brace or tape.

## Season (UK)
- Racing all year; main forest season autumn to spring with big events in spring (Easter multi-day, national championships); summer sprint/urban events. Off 2 gym (runners), pre 2, in 1.

## Tests
- 5 km run: club >22:00, good 18:00-22:00, elite <16:00 (men; practice).
- Y-balance anterior reach difference <4 cm (Plisky 2006, basketball; practice here).
- Single-leg calf raise reps and eyes-closed balance (self, symmetry).

## Peaking
- 10 days: running volume down 40-60%, keep race-pace work, last heavy gym 7 days out.
