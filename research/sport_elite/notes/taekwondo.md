# Taekwondo: research note

## Demands
- Senior World Taekwondo sparring (kyorugi): best of three rounds, 2 min each, 1 min rest. Cadet and junior: 1 min 30 s rounds (Taekwondo Canada G2 2026 rules package, WT format).
- Short high-intensity attacks and counters separated by low-intensity stepping and pauses (time-motion systematic review, Strength Cond J 2020, 42(2):103-121; numbers not available in the abstract I could open).
- Profile review: low body fat, moderate to high aerobic fitness, high lower-limb peak anaerobic power (linked to international success), moderate to high strength, high lower-limb flexibility (Bridge et al. 2014).
- Poomsae (forms): no combat; balance, flexibility, controlled kicks and, in freestyle poomsae, jumping and spinning kicks. Little physiological research found.

## Injuries
- Meta-analysis of competition injuries: pooled 79.3 per 1,000 athlete-exposures (wide CI), no clear effect of age, sex or level (Lystad et al. 2009).
- Australian amateur competitions: 59.9 per 1,000 athlete-exposures, 16.3 per 1,000 min; about a third moderate to severe; upper-limb injuries more often fractures (Lystad et al. 2013).
- High-performance review (8 studies): lower limb most common, especially foot and knee; contusions most frequent, then sprains and ligament tears; direct contact the main cause; overuse more frequent in training; most injuries 1-7 days lost (Cureus 2025).
- Portuguese survey (341 athletes): 90% of injuries in training; foot and fingers 18.9%, thigh, leg and ankle 15.3% each; attacking technique the main mechanism; training sessions over 1 h raised odds (OR 4.2) (Rev Assoc Med Bras 2020).
- Head kicks score 5 points in WT rules, so head impacts and concussion are a real risk in sparring (rule detail: WT 2018).

## Weight making
- Rapid weight loss of 2-10% in the last 2-3 days is common in combat sports and its health harms are documented; authors argue it should be banned (Artioli et al. 2016).
- WT added a random weigh-in 2 h before competition, with a 5% tolerance over the category, to discourage dangerous cutting (WT 2018). No random weigh-in for cadets.
- British Judo (UK governing body, closely related sport): under-18s should not cut weight to qualify; cadets should not use excessive food or fluid restriction; weight management above 2% of natural weight in cadets linked to harm; juniors max 5%; adults only from a fully hydrated state.
- App rule (from these): no dehydration methods for any amateur; no weight cutting at all for under-18s; adults pick a category near natural weight and lose slowly.

## Prevention
- No taekwondo prevention RCT found. Use ankle balance (strong, other sports), landing and knee control (strong, other sports), adductor and hip flexor strength for kicking (practice), neck strength (moderate, collision sports). Protective gear and training-load control (practice; sessions over 1 h linked to injury in the survey).

## Variants
- Sparring: anaerobic power, repeated kicks, reactive footwork.
- Poomsae: balance, flexibility, jump and landing control; less conditioning.

## Season (UK, practice)
- Open tournaments run most of the year. Plan 2-4 peaks around key events (national championships, selections).

## Tests with norms
- FSKT 10 s (max roundhouse kicks in 10 s on a held pad). Men: excellent 24+, good 22-23, regular 18-21. Women: excellent 22+, good 21, regular 17-20 (Santos et al. 2019).
- FSKTmult (5 x 10 s, 10 s rest) total kicks. Men: excellent 108+, good 97-107, regular 85-96. Women: excellent 97+, good 92-96, regular 82-91. Kick decrement index men excellent 3.3% or lower (Santos 2019).
- CMJ and single-leg hop asymmetry: practice bands.

## Evidence limits
- Injury surveillance is decent; prevention and S&C trials are thin. Confidence: moderate.
