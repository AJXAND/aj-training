# Skateboarding: research note (street, park, vert)

## Demands
- Evidence on physical demands is thin. Recreational adults at community skateparks: mean HR 138 bpm, about 72% of age-predicted max, sessions about 65 min, about 70% of the session at moderate intensity or above (Int J Exerc Sci 2020, 13(2):501-510). Hetzler et al. 2011 (Res Q Exerc Sport 82(4):788-793): skateboarding is enough to build or keep aerobic fitness (numbers not in abstract).
- So a session is long intermittent work: short explosive efforts (push, pop, land) with lots of walking back and rest. Power and landing tolerance matter far more than endurance.
- Landing forces: professional skaters dropping from about 46 cm produced peak vertical forces of about 4.5 x body weight (Frederick et al. 2006, as cited in a 2008 NACOB abstract). Secondary source; treat as indicative.
- Variants (practice): street (stairs, rails, gaps: biggest drops and impact, ankle and heel), park and bowl (pumping transitions, carving, repeated runs of about 45 s in contests, higher aerobic and leg-endurance demand, airs), vert (large ramp, high airs, big falls from height, knee and hip impact, slams onto the shoulder and wrist).

## Injuries
- Med Sport Sci 2012 review (vol 58, 142-157): young males most injured; most injuries acute; wrist and forearm most frequent, then lower leg and ankle; mechanism mostly loss of balance and falls, increasingly failed tricks. Prevention evidence inconclusive, but protective equipment and skatepark use recommended.
- Quinn et al. 2024 (Cureus 16(3):e55624), 5026 emergency presentations over 10 years: upper limb 56% of injuries (hand and wrist 57% of those); ankle about 45% of lower-limb injuries; 44% were fractures; concussion or head injury in 723 cases. Falls onto an outstretched hand were the usual mechanism. No helmet data.
- Tokyo 2020 Olympics: skateboarding 21 injuries per 100 athletes, fourth highest, more than double the Games average of 9.1 (Soligard et al. 2023, BJSM).

## Prevention
- Wrist guards: an RCT in 5029 snowboarders cut wrist injuries from 29 to 8 with guards, with no brace-caused injuries (Ronning et al. 2001, AJSM). Systematic review: guards may protect, design unclear (Kim & Lee 2011). Evidence is from snowboarding, graded moderate for skateboarding.
- Helmets: practice and public-health advice (Quinn 2024 recommends helmets and wrist braces).
- Ankle balance training: about 35% fewer sprains, most in those with a previous sprain (Schiftan 2015). Strong in other sports.
- Landing control and knee work (borrowed from jumping sports, practice for skateboarding).
- Falling skill (rolling out, sliding on knee pads in transition) is skill work, not gym, but is the most direct way to cut wrist injury (practice).

## Season (UK, practice)
- Most skaters are not competitive and skate year round; wet UK winters push skaters indoors, often less volume.
- Contest skaters (national series and championships): treat the build to a contest like a tournament.
- Defaults: OFF (wet winter, less skating) gym 2; PRE (spring build) gym 2; IN (summer, heavy skating) gym 1.

## Tests (bands practice)
- CMJ.
- Single-leg balance eyes closed, time each leg.
- Single-leg hop for distance asymmetry.
- Knee-to-wall ankle dorsiflexion (cm), self-comparison.
- Push-up max (wrist and upper body tolerance), self-comparison.
