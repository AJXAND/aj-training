# Ultimate (Ultimate Frisbee): research note

## Demands
- Krustrup & Mohr 2015 (JSCR 29(12):3386-3391), 13 moderately trained male players, GPS + HR: total distance 4.70 km, high-intensity running 0.63 km, sprinting 0.21 km, 17.4 sprints a match. Mean HR 82% HRmax, peak 99%. HIR fell 43-47% in the last 9 min of each half versus the first two periods; second-half HIR about 10% lower. Yo-Yo IR1 1790 m (IR2 657 m); IR2 correlated with late-half HIR (r = 0.69), IR1 with sprint distance (r = 0.74).
- Raya-Gonzalez et al. 2020 (Eur J Hum Mov), mixed official matches: men covered more total, high and very-high speed distance and more medium-high accelerations and decelerations than women. Players active about 35 min of a 62 min match (subs rotate by point).
- Handler vs cutter: one position letter (Raya-Gonzalez 2021, Sci Sports) exists but I could not read its numbers. An undergraduate thesis (Chichester 2021) found no fitness test differences between handlers and cutters. Position differences below are coaching practice: cutters do more repeated sprints, deep cuts and layouts; handlers do more short resets, marking, pivoting and far more throws.
- Talent selection (Connor, Berkelmans & Doma 2023, IJSPP, national youth team): height, 10 m sprint, acceleration and all jumps separated selected from non-selected players. Standing vertical jump cut-off 40.5 cm; run-up one-leg jumps about 37.5-38.5 cm cut-offs. Change of direction did not separate them.
- Beach ultimate: running on soft sand costs about 1.2-1.6 x the energy of grass, walking 1.8-2.7 x; soft sand cuts impact a lot, but jump height is lower and evidence on injury is mixed (Binnie et al. 2014 review, J Sports Sci).

## Injuries
- Fajardo Pulido & Lystad 2020 (Sports 8(12):168), systematic review, 11 studies: incidence 0.4-84.9 per 1000 athlete-exposures; lifetime prevalence of any injury 100%, concussion 26%.
- Lower limb most injured (about 27-88% across studies); knee and thigh most frequent sites; ankle the top single site in several studies (about 21%).
- Types: muscle strains (about 38-41% in two studies), joint sprains (27-28%), contusions. Fractures under 5%.
- Non-contact 40-69% of injuries; contact (mostly player to player) 31-60%. About 80-92% acute onset.
- Authors conclude risk is substantial, especially muscle strains and knee and shoulder sprains, and call for sport-specific prevention programmes. None have been tested.

## Prevention (borrowed from field sports)
- Landing, cutting and deceleration work (FIFA 11+ type): about 50% fewer ACL injuries (Webster & Hewett 2018). Grade strong in related sports, practice for ultimate.
- Nordic hamstring: up to 51% fewer hamstring injuries (van Dyk et al. 2019). Strains are a top injury type.
- Ankle balance: about 35% fewer sprains (Schiftan et al. 2015).
- Shoulder: layouts land on the shoulder; ER and scapular work is practice.

## Variants
- Cutter: repeated sprints, deep cuts, high-point catches, layouts.
- Handler: short bursts, pivots, marking, very high throw counts (forehand flick loads the elbow and wrist, backhand the shoulder and trunk). Practice.
- Beach: sand, 4v4/5v5, lower impact, higher energy cost, more calf and foot load, less elastic jumping.

## Season (UK defaults, practice)
- Outdoor club season (UK Ultimate Tour events) runs late spring to late summer, Nationals at the end of summer or early autumn.
- University season: autumn to spring, with indoor and outdoor events.
- Indoor (5v5) runs over winter; beach events spring and summer.
- Defaults: OFF Oct-Dec (after a 1-2 week break), PRE Jan-Apr, IN May-Sep. Tournaments are 2-day weekends with 3-5 games a day, which is the key recovery challenge.

## Tests (bands are practice, anchored where stated)
- 10 m sprint: field-sport bands.
- CMJ: national youth selection cut-off about 40.5 cm (Connor 2023).
- Yo-Yo IR1: moderately trained men 1790 m mean (Krustrup 2015).
- 505 change of direction both sides.
- Single-leg hop for distance: asymmetry above 10-15% is a flag.
