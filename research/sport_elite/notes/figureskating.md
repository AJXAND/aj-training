# Figure skating: research note

## Demands
- Free skate energetics (11 skaters, ECSS 2012 abstract): about 74% aerobic, 12% anaerobic lactic, 14% anaerobic alactic. On-ice peak HR 194 bpm, lactate 9.7 mmol/L, VO2max about 59 ml/kg/min.
- A Routledge book chapter notes skating energetics have barely been studied directly; estimates borrow from 800-1500 m running and speed skating (Science of Figure Skating 2018).
- Jump landings: impact rose with extra revolutions, ranging 3 to 9 x body weight (Lockwood & Gervais, cited in Lockwood, Baudin & Gervais).
- Needs: vertical take-off power, fast rotation and the trunk stiffness to hold it, single-leg landing control on a stiff boot, edge control, flexibility. Pairs men lift and throw partners overhead.

## Injuries
- Review (Han, Geminiani, Micheli 2018): acute and overuse injuries in similar proportions overall. Overuse more common in singles; acute more common in pairs, dance and synchro. Lower limb dominates in all disciplines; pairs accounts for most upper-limb injuries. Ankle sprain most common injury; patellar tendinopathy most common overuse injury; stress fractures most common overuse injury in female singles. Recommends well-fitted boots, intrinsic foot and ankle strength, lower-limb flexibility.
- Systematic review (29 studies, MSc dissertation, PROSPERO registered): prevalence 2.1-34%, incidence 1.72 per 1,000 h on-ice, mainly knee, foot and ankle. Risk factors: older age, previous stress fracture, higher body mass, more than 12 sessions a week, skipping meals, RED-S indicators, tight hamstrings and quads, more on-ice time, boot-foot length mismatch. Evidence rated poor (Schmidt 2023).

## Prevention
- No skating prevention RCT found. Use ankle balance and landing programmes (strong evidence in other sports), patellar tendon loading (moderate for management), lumbar capacity, adequate energy intake (RED-S), boot fit, jump-count management (practice).

## Variants
- Singles: most jumps, most overuse, stress fractures; power and landing capacity.
- Pairs: lifts, throws, twists; men need upper-body and overhead strength, women throw landings and catch positions.
- Ice dance: no multi-revolution jumps; lifts, edges, footwork, longer continuous effort.

## Season (UK, practice)
- ISU season runs roughly late summer to March (Grand Prix autumn, British Championships winter, Worlds March). Off-season April to June, when new programmes are built.

## Tests with norms
- CMJ, single-leg hop and stick, Y-balance, side plank hold. I found no published skating norms I could verify; bands are practice. Y-balance anterior difference above 4 cm is a flag (Plisky 2006, Part 0).

## Evidence limits
- Weak. Mostly descriptive injury data and one energetics abstract. Confidence: limited.
