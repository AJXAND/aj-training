# Cheerleading (base, flyer, back spot)

## Demands
- Competition routines under 3 minutes (Gavanda et al. 2023), combining stunts, pyramids, basket tosses, standing and running tumbling, jumps and dance. Near-maximal effort with short intense bursts.
- Bases: lift, press and catch a flyer overhead; big squat, overhead push, trunk stiffness and wrist load.
- Flyers: balance on one leg on bases' hands, body tension, twisting dismounts and landings from height.
- Back spots: control the flyer's ankles or hips, catch head and shoulders in cradles; lower and upper strength, quick reactions.
- Training load is high before championships: elite teams trained 150-270 min a day and entered competition not fully recovered (Gavanda 2023).

## Injuries
- US high schools (Currie et al. 2016, Pediatrics): concussion the most common injury (31.1%); most injuries during stunts, often dismounts; cheer ranked 18th of 22 sports for injury rate but second for proportion of injuries costing 3+ weeks.
- Shields et al. 2009 (J Athl Train): stunts caused 60% of injuries and 96% of concussions or closed head injuries; spotting or basing caused 34% of stunt injuries; ankle most injured (12%); ankle, low back and wrist most often re-injured.
- AAP policy 2012 (Brenner et al.): lower-limb sprains and strains most common; cheer causes about 66% of catastrophic injuries in high school girls; risk factors include higher BMI, previous injury, hard surfaces, stunts and less trained coaches.

## Prevention and rules (graded)
- Concussion (rule): remove at once; no return same day; written clearance before return (AAP 2012). UK grassroots guidance 2023: no sport for 24 h, graded return, no contact-risk activity for at least 21 days after a confirmed concussion (Sport England page). Treat stunting as contact-risk.
- Stunting safety (rule/consensus): spotters for pyramids, pyramids 2 people high, no stunts on hard, wet or uneven surfaces, skill progressions before advancing, certified coaches (AAP 2012; Shields 2009).
- Modules: `ankle` (strong across sports), `landing` (strong in jump sports), `wrist` (practice), `lowback` (practice; bases), `shoulderER` and `scap` (practice; bases and tumbling), `neck` (moderate in collision sports, practice here).
- Strength and conditioning for bases and spotters: recommended by Shields 2009 and AAP 2012 (consensus).
- Load: avoid long full-out stunting days back to back before competitions; rest day before competition (Gavanda 2023).

## Variants
- Base: max strength, overhead push, wrist, low back.
- Flyer: balance, landing, ankle, trunk tension, low body mass emphasis is a coach decision, not the app's.
- Back spot: lower and upper strength, neck and catch mechanics.
- Tumbler (all roles): wrist, ankle, Achilles, landing.

## Season (UK)
- All Star and university comps run mainly autumn to spring with major nationals in spring (practice). Off June-August, pre September-December, in January-May.
- Gym: off 3, pre 2, in 2.

## Tests (bands practice; no published cheer norms verified)
- CMJ, overhead press or push press x body mass, single-leg hop and stick, wall handstand hold, single-leg balance.

## Peaking
- 7-10 days: cut gym volume 50%, keep intensity, last heavy session 4-5 days out, rest day before competition.
