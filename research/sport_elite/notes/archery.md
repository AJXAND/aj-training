# Archery (recurve, compound, barebow)

## Demands
- Static, repeated holds. A World Archery 720 round is 72 arrows; club and squad days often reach 100-200 arrows with practice ends (practice).
- Each shot: raise, draw, anchor, hold a few seconds, release. Bow arm works in sustained abduction (deltoid, rotator cuff, lower trapezius, serratus). Draw arm works through scapular retraction (rhomboids, middle trapezius, posterior deltoid) and finger flexors on the string (recurve, barebow).
- Compound: release aid and let-off cut holding force at full draw, but peak draw weight is usually higher (practice).
- Energy: low. Aerobic fitness supports long tournament days walking to targets (outdoor 70 m recurve, 50 m compound) and steadier heart rate and breathing (practice).
- Performance limiters: hold steadiness, postural sway, local muscular endurance of the shoulder girdle, consistent release. Evidence on gym training improving score is thin (practice).

## Injuries
- Shoulder pain is the dominant problem. Grover and Sinha 2017 (156 Indian competitive archers): point prevalence of shoulder pain 54%, lifetime 83%, recurrent 65%. Past shoulder pain was the main associated factor (OR 2.57). Arrows per session and training hours were not associated in this cross-sectional design.
- Emergency department data (US NEISS 2013-2022, Konda et al. 2023): finger injuries 66% in men, mostly lacerations from string or arrow handling. These are equipment and technique issues, not trainable.
- Asymmetry: Schmitt et al. 2021 found unilateral archers had more total rotation (mainly internal) on the pull-arm side and more scapular upward rotation than bilateral archers; authors suggest bilateral work may help prevent this.
- Other: draw-arm elbow and forearm tendon pain, neck and upper back pain from anchor posture, low back from long standing (practice).

## Prevention (graded)
1. `shoulderER` rotator cuff ER and IR strength, both arms: moderate by analogy with overhead sports (Andersson 2017 handball), practice in archery.
2. `scap` lower trapezius and serratus: practice.
3. `tspine` thoracic rotation and extension: practice.
4. `elbow` forearm and grip capacity on the draw hand: practice.
5. `lowback` trunk endurance for long standing days: practice.
6. Load: build arrow counts gradually after breaks; most shoulder pain starts after load spikes (practice). Correct draw weight for strength.
7. Contralateral or reverse-side band draws for balance (Schmitt 2021 suggestion, not trialled).

## Variants
- Recurve: full holding load at anchor and clicker; finger tab on string; highest scapular endurance need.
- Compound: let-off lowers holding force, higher peak draw, release aid; trigger control and shoulder.
- Barebow: string walking and face walking change anchor; similar to recurve without sight.

## Season (UK)
- Outdoor season April to September (WA and imperial rounds); indoor season October to March (Portsmouth, WA 18 m). Many archers shoot all year.
- Gym: off 3, pre 2, in 2 (low recovery cost, so 2 is fine in season).

## Tests (no published norms for most; bands are practice)
- Inverted row or pull-up max reps.
- Side plank hold.
- Single-leg balance eyes closed.
- Full-draw hold with a training band (s).
- Shoulder ER:IR strength ratio about 0.66 or more (Part 0 shared rule; overhead sport data).

## Peaking
- 5-7 days: keep shooting volume steady, drop gym volume 50%, no new exercises, no heavy pulling within 48 h.
