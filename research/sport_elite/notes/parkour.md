# Parkour and freerunning: research note

## Demands
- Parkour: efficient movement over obstacles (precision jumps, vaults, cat leaps, climb-ups, drops). Freerunning/freestyle: adds flips and spins. FIG competitions: speed run and freestyle.
- Short maximal efforts linked into 20-60 s runs; long sessions. High jump power, landing forces, pulling and grip strength, wrist loading.
- Landing: precision and roll landings reduce peak vertical force by ~38-43% (about 3 BW vs 5.2 BW) and loading rate by ~54-63% versus a traditional drop landing; time to peak force doubles (Puddle & Maulder 2013, 10 male traceurs).
- Performance (15 traceurs): CMJ with arms 56 cm, standing long jump 273 cm, 40 m 5.1 s, T-test 10.8 s (Elizondo-Donado 2026).

## Injuries
- Survey of 180 French traceurs: 1.7 lower-limb injuries per 1,000 h; ankle 2.3 (mostly sprains, 47% recurrence), knee 1.1 (contusions, sprains). No warm-up: more knee injuries; no physical conditioning: more ankle and knee injuries; minimalist shoes: more knee injuries. Fatigue blamed by 60% (Grospretre & El Khattabi 2022, retrospective).
- Madrid survey (161): 70% injured in 12 months; lower limb 70%, upper limb 25%; ankle 28.5%, knee 18.4%, wrist 12.6%; sprains and contusions; precision jumps most common movement (Giner Gran 2020).

## Prevention (graded)
- Landing training and ankle balance (strong in team sports; practice here), conditioning (moderate association), warm-up with real mechanical load (moderate association), both-sided training (practice), gradual footwear transition (moderate association), wrist strength (practice), patellar tendon care (practice).

## Season
- No formal season; jams and competitions. Off 3, pre 2, in 2.

## Tests (bands anchored to the 15-traceur means; practice)
- CMJ: club <45, good 45-60, elite >60 cm. Standing long jump: <240, 240-280, >280 cm. 40 m: >5.5, 5.0-5.5, <5.0 s. Pull-ups and dead hang (practice).

## Peaking
- 7 days: last heavy legs 5-7 days out, drop-landing volume halved.
