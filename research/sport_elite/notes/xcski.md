# Cross-country skiing: research note (classic, skate, roller ski)

## Demands
- Sandbakk & Holmberg 2017 (IJSPP 12(8):1003-1011): among the most demanding endurance sports; varied terrain, several sub-techniques loading upper and lower body differently; race speeds have risen more than in any other winter Olympic sport. Formats: sprint (about 3-4 min heats), distance (roughly 10-50 km), mass start and skiathlon.
- World-class VO2max: above 80 ml/kg/min men, about 70 women (Solli, Tonnessen & Sandbakk 2017, Front Physiol). 17 world-class skiers as seniors: men 81.6, women 71.3 ml/kg/min; 861 h a year of training, strength 55 h a year (about 6%); skiing plus roller skiing 68% of endurance time (Walther et al. 2023, Front Physiol).
- The most successful female skier: 937 h a year, 92% low intensity by time, strength training 75 h a year (about 8%), heavy sessions with 3-4 upper-body exercises (Solli 2017).
- Strength evidence: heavy upper-body strength training improved double-poling economy and time to exhaustion (Osteras, Helgerud & Hoff 2002); maximal strength training improved double-poling work economy in trained female skiers (Hoff et al. 1999, MSSE 31(6):870-877). Losnegard et al. (ECSS 2009 abstract): 12 weeks heavy strength twice a week raised pull-down 1RM 19% and half squat 12%, skating VO2max 7%, but 5 min roller-ski time-trial gains matched controls. So strength helps economy and upper-body power; race-performance gains are less clear.
- Classic vs skate (practice): classic leans on double poling (lats, triceps, trunk flexion) and diagonal stride (kick, hip extension); skate is a lateral push off an edged ski (hip abductors and adductors, single-leg strength, more leg power) with more upper-body pushing per stroke.

## Injuries
- FIS World Cup surveillance (Florenes et al. 2012, SJMSS 22(1):58-66): XC had the lowest rate of the ski disciplines, 6.3 time-loss and 0.7 severe injuries per 100 athletes per season (alpine 29.8, snowboard 37.8).
- Lower back the most injured site in XC World Cup seasons 2006-08 (25%); more than 550 h a year training linked to low back pain; increased thoracic kyphosis linked to back pain in young elite skiers (Xu et al. 2021 review, Front Physiol).
- Prospective cohort of collegiate-level skiers (Worth, Reid & Henry 2017, BJSM abstract): 3.8 injuries per 1000 h; lower limb 2.13, upper 0.46, trunk 0.22; overuse 2.76 vs acute 1.05; off-season higher than in-season (5.25 vs 2.27, p = 0.07); previous injury the only significant predictor; running time trended up.
- Finnish elite: most skier injuries happened in other sports (dryland), commonest acute site the ankle (Ristolainen 2011 thesis).
- Roller skiing (practice): falls on tarmac (wrist, elbow, shoulder, skin), no snow to slow you, poor braking; helmet, gloves, quiet roads. No epidemiology found.

## Prevention
- Lumbar and trunk capacity (practice/moderate): back extensions, reverse hyper, side plank, bird dog.
- Shoulder: high poling volume; ER and scapular work (practice).
- Lateral hip (skate): abductor and Copenhagen work (practice, groin evidence from football).
- Ankle balance: ankle the commonest acute site in dryland (strong in other sports).
- Load: off-season running drives overuse; ramp running gradually (Worth trend).

## Season (UK, practice)
- Snow season Dec-Mar, usually trips abroad. UK athletes do most specific work on roller skis May-Oct (British Rollerski series runs through summer).
- Defaults: OFF Apr-Jun (gym 3, heaviest strength), PRE Jul-Nov (gym 2, roller ski specific, more power), IN Dec-Mar (gym 1).

## Tests (bands practice)
- Max pull-ups.
- CMJ.
- Double-pole or SkiErg 1000 m time (self-comparison; Concept2 rankings exist but not verified here).
- Back extension hold (Biering-Sorensen style, self-comparison).
- Lateral bound for distance each side (skate, symmetry).
